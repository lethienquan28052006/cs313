# Figures (paper-style) — what each one shows

All figures come from the **official D-Tracker code** (commit `11c181d`), run as in the authors' `bin/run.sh` (init=0, rankupdate=1, outlier=1, stl=0). Regenerate with `python figs/make_figs.py`. PNG (slides) and PDF (report) are in `figures/`. Country outlines: Natural Earth 1:110m (public domain).

For the parameter names (W(core), W(key), W(loc), A, D, dk, dl), see `HYPERPARAMETERS.md`.

---

## Fig. 1(a) — `fig1a_device_fit` — does the model fit the data stream?

- One panel per keyword of the **Device** dataset (8 keywords). Gray = real search volume (average of 48 countries), blue = D-Tracker's fit.
- Dotted vertical lines = **model switches** (12 in our run): moments where MDL decided a new model explains the newest 2 years better than the old one.
- **How to read it:** blue follows gray closely, including the rise and fall of Galaxy and Xperia and the yearly iPhone spikes. Fits are stitched from the successive models, so small jumps appear at switch lines.
- The paper shows the same idea in one panel; we split by keyword so it is readable on a slide.

## Fig. 1(b) — `fig1b_device_2010`, `fig1b_device_2019` — what is inside one model?

Each figure shows **one fitted model** (one 2-year window):

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| Top row | **Latent dynamics W(core)**, one panel per location group. Thick line = curve generated by the reaction–diffusion equation, thin = raw fitted factor |
| Bottom left | **Keyword factor W(key)**: which keywords belong to each dynamic (1.00 = strongest, per column) |
| Bottom middle | **Location factor W(loc)** on a world map: each country coloured by the group it belongs to most |
| Bottom right | **Diffusion** between groups: an arrow Gj → Gg means interest in dynamic #i spreads from group j to group g (d = strength) |

**What our run shows vs the paper:**
- **2010–2011** (dk=3, dl=2, same sizes as the paper): Group G2 = India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Iran, Poland, Czechia, Romania (8 countries). In G2, Dynamics #2 (= **Galaxy**) rises fast, as in the paper. Dynamics #3 (= **iPad**) diffuses G1 → G2, like the paper's "diffusion from Group 1 to Group 2 for Galaxy and iPad".
- **2019–2021**: the model grew to dk=4, dl=4 (RankUpdate). Dynamics #1 = iPhone, flowing out of G1 (South America, parts of Asia and Africa) with strength 0.34. The paper's "iPhone diffuses from US/Europe/China to the rest of the world" is **not** reproduced exactly: our group split differs (US/Europe/China fall in G4 here).

## Fig. 3 — `fig3_ablation` — does each part of the model help?

- For each dataset, the full model is compared with 4 versions that each **remove one part**: diffusion, seasonal, outlier, RankUpdate (24 extra runs, `run_ablation.sh`).
- Bar = **(MAE without the part − MAE of the full model) / MAE without the part**, at a 26-week horizon (Covid: 14 days). **Above 0 = the part helps; below 0 = removing it would have been better.**
- Numbers: `results/ablation/fig3_ablation.json`.

| Part | Paper says | Our run |
|---|---|---|
| Diffusion | big help on VoD and Chatapp | **Chatapp +10.2 %** ✔; VoD only +1.4 %; slightly negative on Pythonlib |
| Seasonal | helps Device, Language, Covid | Device +8.5 % ✔, Chatapp +7 %; **hurts** Language (−5.5 %) and Covid (−9.3 %, the Covid run uses period 52 on daily data) |
| Outlier | helps some datasets | Pythonlib +8.8 %, Covid +3.9 %; small or negative elsewhere ✔ |
| RankUpdate | helps over the whole stream | **helps everywhere** (+1.9 % to +19.4 %); without it Covid breaks down (MAE 10.9 vs 0.9, bar cut at 25 %) ✔ |

## Fig. 5 — `fig5a_pythonlib_2013`, `fig5b_pythonlib_2020` — Pythonlib in two periods

Same layout as Fig. 1(b). Our run happens to switch models at almost exactly the paper's periods (windows starting 2013-05-19 and 2020-02-16).

- **(a) 2013–2015:** each of numpy, pandas and sklearn gets its own dynamic. Diffusion goes **G2 → G1** for dynamics #2 (pandas, d=0.64) and #1 (numpy, d=0.43). Here G2 = 35 countries including the US and Australia, and G1 = China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Russia, Germany, the Nordics and others. So pandas interest flows **from the US-led group into the China-led group**, the same story as the paper ("Group 1, e.g. North America and Australia, → Group 2, Asia mainly China") ✔. The group numbers are swapped and the exact members differ.
- **(b) 2020–2022:** **China forms a group of its own (G1)**. In China, Dynamics #1 (= **pytorch**, also numpy/pandas) **rises**, while Dynamics #3 (= **tensorflow**) **falls**. This is the paper's "shift from tensorflow to pytorch" ✔. Diffusion G1 → G2 for Dynamics #2 (pandas/numpy/matplotlib). The paper has the same keywords but the opposite direction (G2 → G1).

## Extra — `table3_mae` — Table 3 at a glance

MAE at the middle horizon for every method, per dataset. Blue = D-Tracker (paper value and our two runs), gray = baselines we ran, **orange = naive "repeat the last value"**, which is not in the paper. Bars longer than the panel are cut and labelled.

**Key observation:** the naive forecast has lower MAE than D-Tracker in 17 of 18 rows, both against our runs **and** against the paper's own numbers. PatchTST is close to naive. We checked that our PatchTST/Autoformer/CoST wrappers do **not** use future data (`bench/test_leakage.py`: scrambling the future changes the forecast by exactly 0).
